## INTRO

Hey folks, it's Chinmay, here with Issa, Managing Partner at Ambrosia Ventures. Over fifty biopharma M&As in the first half of 2026 — exits are back. Vertex just scooped up Crinetics for ten billion, the biggest deal they've ever done. They've also struck licensing deals with Halozyme, Ono, and Zai Lab. This is Hot Off The Press, where we break down the latest M&As, licensing deals and exits. Let's go.

## CRINETICS — $10B

**CHINMAY**
July 6th. Eighty-five a share, all cash. Ten billion, or eight point eight net of cash. That's double their previous record, which was Alpine in 2024 at four point nine. They get PALSONIFY, an oral acromegaly drug approved last September, and atumelnant, in Phase 3 for congenital adrenal hyperplasia. Vertex is guiding to five billion in combined peak sales. Shareholders approved Friday, closes around September 1st.

Issa, you told me this was the weirdest good deal of the year.

**ISSA**
Because of how the price got set. Read the background section of the proxy.

Vertex opens at seventy-eight in March. Rejected. Comes back at eighty-three in April. Rejected. So Crinetics goes shopping — six parties, and every one of them walks. The most serious one can't get above six billion.

So now there's no auction. No second bidder. Nobody to leak to. And Vertex raises itself anyway. Eighty-four fifty, then eighty-five, best and final. They bid against themselves. Twice.

And eighty-five is a hundred and two percent premium. We benchmark this in Solidus, our deal intelligence platform — fifty to a hundred percent is the normal band. So Vertex paid the ceiling of the market with nobody else in the room.

**CHINMAY**
Structure's clean, too. All cash. No stock, no CVR, no earnout. They went in with thirteen point six billion and zero long-term debt, and they're partly funding it with a four and a half billion term loan — first real borrowing in company history.

Two other things. Alpine back in 2024 got booked as an asset acquisition, so four point four billion hit the income statement in a single quarter. And on the FTC side, a court threw out the expanded HSR form in February, so this deal filed on the old paperwork. Cleared mid-August, no second request.

Then Friday's vote: seventy-nine million shares for, thirteen thousand against. But the golden parachute vote failed.

**ISSA**
Four fast reads.

One, no CVR is the tell. Atumelnant is Phase 3 and unproven — that's the textbook place to put one. Solidus has sixteen hundred verified deals in it, pulled straight from SEC and HSR filings, and contingent consideration shows up exactly where the two sides argue about probability. Vertex didn't argue.

Two, that debt ladder is 364 days, two years, three years. That's a company planning to be debt-free again quickly.

Three, don't get fooled by the accounting. Alpine cratered one quarter of GAAP. Crinetics has an approved product attached, so I'd expect it to book as a business combination instead — spread over years, no crater. Same cash, totally different financials. That's my read, they haven't confirmed it.

Four, the vote. Ninety-nine point nine eight percent said yes to the price. The same shareholders said no to management's payout. Non-binding — but it means the change-of-control package got richer faster than the stock did.

## HALOZYME — $15M

**CHINMAY**
April 7th, completely different posture. Vertex licenses Halozyme's Hypercon — microparticle tech that hyperconcentrates a biologic so you can inject it at home instead of sitting in an infusion center. Global exclusive, up to three targets. Fifteen million upfront plus milestones and royalties.

Fifteen million against a ten billion dollar acquisition. Rounding error, or strategy?

**ISSA**
Both. And here's what I love — fifteen million is *exactly* the median upfront for a discovery-stage deal in our data. Dead on. Vertex paid the going rate for one preclinical program and got a platform across three targets.

And you're not buying the technology. You're buying the right to use it, at a price where you don't have to be correct. Halozyme only gets paid if Vertex wins.

**CHINMAY**
But Vertex is a small molecule house. Cystic fibrosis, the pain program — that's pills.

**ISSA**
Which is exactly the tell. You don't license high-concentration *biologics* delivery for a portfolio you don't have. Povetacicept's a fusion protein. They're building plumbing for an injectable, chronic, at-home future, years before they need it. Cheapest strategic option anybody bought this year.

---

## ONO — Japan & Korea

**CHINMAY**
June last year. Vertex licenses povetacicept to Ono for Japan and South Korea, in IgA nephropathy. And the money flows *toward* Vertex — upfront, milestones, tiered royalties. Ono runs the filings and commercializes. Terms undisclosed.

Why hand over the territory at all?

**ISSA**
They're renting out rooms they were never going to live in. Remember where the molecule came from — Vertex paid four point nine billion for Alpine in 2024, and povetacicept *was* that deal.

So the question afterward is what you do with the world where you have no infrastructure. They were never building a nephrology field force in Japan. So convert it into cash and royalties, keep the US and Europe where the value sits. That's portfolio management, not retreat.

**CHINMAY**
Why Japan specifically, though? Feels small for a kidney drug.

**ISSA**
It's the opposite. Japan runs mandatory urinalysis screening through the school system, has for decades, and the literature says seventy to eighty percent of detected cases get caught that way. They find IgA nephropathy earlier than anywhere on earth. Patients already identified, nephrologists concentrated.

And on the undisclosed terms — I won't guess a number, regional deals price way below the global comps. But the shape is standard: small upfront, milestones carrying sixty to seventy-five percent of total value.

## ZAI LAB — Greater China

**CHINMAY**
January 2025, same molecule. Zai Lab gets mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan and Singapore. Same structure, Vertex collecting.

Is China big enough to matter here, or is it a nice-to-have?

**ISSA**
Biggest there is. China has the largest absolute IgA nephropathy population in the world.

But watch the direction. For three years the story's been Western pharma *in*-licensing Chinese assets. This is the reverse. And Zai Lab has regulatory and commercial infrastructure a US biotech can't replicate. You're not buying a distributor, you're buying five years.

**CHINMAY**
Put Ono and Zai Lab together for me.

**ISSA**
One move, not two. In five months Vertex regionalized an entire acquired asset — China, Singapore, Japan, Korea — and kept the US and Europe outright.

Buy the global asset. Keep the two markets that carry the economics. Monetize the rest. Every one of those deals chips away at what Alpine cost them. Do that twice in a year with the same molecule and there's a memo behind it.

## CLOSEOUT

**ISSA**
The thread is that Vertex is really disciplined about where it puts principal at risk. One enormous check — all cash, no contingency, no competing bidder — for the thing they were certain about. Everywhere else they refused. Fifteen million for an option. And on the Asia deals they weren't paying at all, they were collecting.

Cash where you're sure. Structure where you're not.

**CHINMAY**
And if you're a founder listening to this?

**ISSA**
Then it's in the comps. Which buyers actually paid cash. Where CVRs showed up. What a premium looks like with a real auction versus one bidder in the room.

That's why we built Solidus. Every number I quoted today came out of it, and every one traces back to a filing. It's at solidus dot ambrosiaventures dot co, free to start.

And upfronts are up twenty to thirty percent since 2022. Negotiate off a comp you remember from a few years ago and you're negotiating against yourself.

**CHINMAY**
Vertex just showed us what conviction costs. Thanks Issa. Catch us on the next segment of Hot Off The Press.
