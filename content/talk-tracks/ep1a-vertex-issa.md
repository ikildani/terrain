# Hot Off The Press — Episode 1a: Vertex
## Full two-hander talk track — Chinmay & Issa

**Record:** week of Aug 31, 2026
**Note on delivery:** this is written to be *talked*, not read. Nothing here is a paragraph anyone should recite word-for-word. Hit the numbers exactly, let everything else drift. The interruptions and the half-sentences are on purpose — leave them in.

And the biggest one of 2026 is closing basically as we're recording this. Vertex is buying Crinetics. Ten billion dollars, all cash. They've also been quietly doing licensing deals — Halozyme back in April, Ono and Zai Lab last year.

So today on Hot Off The Press we're pulling all of it apart. Issa, where do you want to start?

**ISSA**
Honestly? The Crinetics deal is the weirdest good deal I've looked at this year.

**CHINMAY**
Weird how?

**ISSA**
I'll show you. Set it up first.

## CRINETICS — the $10 billion one

**CHINMAY** *(facts)*
Okay. July 6th. Vertex agrees to buy Crinetics Pharmaceuticals for eighty-five dollars a share in cash. About ten billion on the equity, or eight point eight billion net of the cash Crinetics is sitting on.

This is far and away the biggest thing Vertex has ever done. Their previous record was Alpine Immune Sciences in 2024 at four point nine billion, so this is double.

What they're getting: PALSONIFY, which is an oral drug for acromegaly, FDA approved in September of last year. And atumelnant, which is in Phase 3 for congenital adrenal hyperplasia. Vertex is saying roughly five billion in combined peak annual sales. And strategically it makes endocrinology a whole new pillar for them, next to cystic fibrosis, pain, blood disorders, kidney.

**ISSA**
Right. So here's the weird part.

Go read the background-of-the-merger section in the proxy. It's maybe six pages and it's the most interesting thing anybody filed this summer.

Vertex opens at seventy-eight a share in late March. Crinetics board turns it down. Vertex comes back in April at eighty-three. Board turns that down too, and tells their bankers, fine, go see who else is out there.

**CHINMAY**
And?

**ISSA**
Nobody's out there.

They line up six potential counterparties. One of them, the proxy calls them Party A, actually digs in. And then around the first of May they walk, because they can't get themselves above six billion dollars. The other two that were still circling drop out the following week.

So now it's May. There is no auction. There is no second bidder. There's nobody to leak to. And Vertex... raises their own bid. Eighty-four fifty. Crinetics counters at eighty-seven, Vertex says eighty-five, best and final, board takes it in June.

**CHINMAY**
Wait, so they bid against themselves.

**ISSA**
They bid against themselves. Twice. And the eighty-five is a hundred and two percent premium.

**CHINMAY**
That's got to be one of the biggest premiums of the year.

**ISSA**
It is. And I can put a number on that, because we benchmark this stuff in Solidus, which is our deal intelligence platform. Across the acquisitions in there, fifty to a hundred percent is the normal premium band. So a hundred and two isn't off the map, but it's the ceiling of the map. That's the top end of what buyers pay when they *do* have competition.

**CHINMAY**
And here there wasn't any.

**ISSA**
And here there wasn't any. Which is the thing I keep chewing on.

Though I want to be careful, because people are going to hear "sole bidder, huge premium" and go straight to "they overpaid." That's lazy. When there's no auction, the price isn't telling you what the asset is worth on the open market. It's telling you what it's worth to Vertex specifically. Those are just different numbers.

**CHINMAY**
Okay, but is it worth it? Like actually. What did ten billion dollars buy?

**ISSA**
So this is where I think both sides are being sloppy.

What you're buying is one product that's selling and one that isn't approved yet. PALSONIFY did five million bucks in Q4 last year. Ten point three in Q1. Twenty-four million in Q2.

**CHINMAY**
That's a real ramp though.

**ISSA**
It's a great ramp. I'm not knocking it, the launch is working. But annualize Q2 and you're at, call it a hundred million a year. Against eight point eight billion net.

**CHINMAY**
Which is what, eighty-something times revenue.

**ISSA**
Something like that, yeah, and it's not a useful number. Nobody's buying this on a revenue multiple.

The other half of the thesis is atumelnant, and that's a Phase 3 asset. They dosed the first patient in the pivotal adult trial in December, started the pediatric study in January. That is not de-risked. That's a readout sitting out there in the future that either works or doesn't.

**CHINMAY**
So what are they actually paying for?

**ISSA**
Time. I think they're buying time.

If the five billion peak number is real, two times peak sales is a totally normal multiple in this business. Stifel and William Blair both basically said that — rich, but defensible *if* the peak number happens. What I'd point at is Vertex's own guidance. They told investors this becomes accretive to non-GAAP operating income in 2029.

**CHINMAY**
2029.

**ISSA**
Three years out. Nobody writes a ten billion dollar check for something that starts paying you in three years unless the thing you're actually worried about is what your revenue looks like in the 2030s. This is a patent cliff purchase. It's just not being described that way.

**CHINMAY**
Let's talk structure, because you flagged something to me before we started about there being no CVR.

**ISSA**
Yeah, and that's the part I keep turning over.

All cash. No stock. No contingent value right, no earnout, nothing.

**CHINMAY**
Remind people what a CVR is.

**ISSA**
It's a bet you bolt onto the deal when the two sides can't agree. Buyer thinks the drug's a coin flip, seller thinks it's a sure thing, so you say fine — you get X now, and another Y if it gets approved. Splits the difference on something nobody can prove yet.

And there was an obvious one to write here. Atumelnant. Put a CVR on the CAH readout, everybody goes home happy. That's textbook.

**CHINMAY**
And they just... didn't.

**ISSA**
They just didn't. And they paid a hundred and two percent premium on top of not doing it.

Solidus has sixteen hundred-plus verified transactions in it, going back to 2017, and the pattern is really consistent — contingent consideration shows up exactly where the two sides are arguing about probability. So when a buyer skips it in a spot where it's practically begging to be used, that's them saying: we're not arguing. We think we know.

**CHINMAY**
Where do those sixteen hundred come from?

**ISSA**
SEC EDGAR, HSR pre-merger filings, ClinicalTrials.gov, the wires. All filings, nothing scraped, nothing estimated. Which matters for exactly this question, because "did the deal have a CVR" is not something you can guess at. It's either in the agreement or it isn't.

**CHINMAY**
How are they paying for it? Ten billion is a lot of cash.

**ISSA**
This is my favorite detail in the whole thing, actually. Pull up their June 30th balance sheet. Thirteen point six billion in cash and marketable securities, and zero long-term debt.

**CHINMAY**
Zero?

**ISSA**
Zero. Vertex has never really been a borrower. And now they're doing their first big one. On announcement day they lined up four and a half billion of committed bridge financing, Bank of America and Morgan Stanley. Then July 30th they killed the bridge and replaced it with an actual term loan — four and a half billion, senior unsecured, delayed draw, split into three tranches. A billion due in 364 days, a billion at two years, two and a half at three years. Hadn't drawn a dollar of it as of the last 10-Q.

**CHINMAY**
Why does the tranching matter?

**ISSA**
Because look at the shape of it. They've built themselves a three-year ladder to pay the whole thing off out of operating cash flow. That's not a company that wants to be levered. That's a company borrowing on the way to a specific date and planning to be done.

**CHINMAY**
Something you said earlier about the 10-K — you think people are going to get this wrong.

**ISSA**
I think a lot of people are going to get this wrong, and it's worth ninety seconds.

Go back to Alpine, 2024. Four point nine billion, and it got structured as an *asset* acquisition. Which means four point four billion of it went straight through the income statement in one quarter as acquired in-process R&D. Just torched the GAAP numbers for that period.

Crinetics is a different animal, because there's an approved product and an actual commercial organization attached to it. So I'd expect this one to get treated as a business combination instead — goodwill and intangibles capitalized on the balance sheet, amortized out over years. No single-quarter crater.

**CHINMAY**
Same money out the door though.

**ISSA**
Exactly the same money. Totally different-looking financials. And Vertex hasn't confirmed the treatment yet, to be clear, that's my read. But if it goes that way and you're sitting there comparing Vertex's GAAP earnings from 2024 to 2026 to 2027 without adjusting for it, you will draw the exact wrong conclusion about that company.

**CHINMAY**
FTC side. Anything there?

**ISSA**
Antitrust-wise, no. There's no overlap. Cystic fibrosis, pain, blood, kidney on one side, acromegaly and adrenal disease on the other. Nothing to fight about. Waiting period ran out mid-August, no second request, and they've cleared Austria, Germany and Australia.

But there's a wrinkle that I think is genuinely under-covered.

**CHINMAY**
Go.

**ISSA**
So, two years ago the FTC rewrote the HSR form. First real overhaul since 1978. Way more documents, narratives about overlaps, all of it. Took effect February 2025 and everybody in the deal bar spent last year complaining about how much work it was.

This past February, a federal court in East Texas vacated it. Threw the whole rule out. FTC asked the Fifth Circuit for a stay, Fifth Circuit said no. So right now the agencies are back to taking the old form.

**CHINMAY**
So the biggest biopharma deal of the year filed on the old paperwork.

**ISSA**
On the pre-2025 form, yeah. Filing fee's still the top tier, two point four six million dollars just to hand it in, but the burden was a fraction of what it would've been a year ago. And if you're a founder or a BD person listening to this, that window may not last. The FTC's appealing.

**CHINMAY**
Last thing on Crinetics. Shareholders voted Friday.

**ISSA**
They did, and there's a split in that vote that nobody's written about.

The merger passes with seventy-nine point two million shares for, and thirteen thousand six hundred against.

**CHINMAY**
Thirteen thousand. Out of seventy-nine million.

**ISSA**
Ninety-nine point nine eight percent. Nobody was conflicted about eighty-five dollars in cash.

But — same ballot, second question, the advisory vote on golden parachute pay for management. That one *failed*. Roughly thirty-four million for, forty-five million against.

**CHINMAY**
Huh.

**ISSA**
It's non-binding, doesn't affect the closing, most people will scroll right past it. I wouldn't. That's a shareholder base saying we love the price and we do not love what the executive team is collecting on the way out the door. Usually means the change-of-control package got richer faster than the stock did.

And it's a decent reminder for founders — while your bankers are negotiating the deal, your comp committee is negotiating a second one, and your shareholders can see both.

**CHINMAY**
Closing?

**ISSA**
On or about September 1st. So, now.

## HALOZYME — the $15 million one

**CHINMAY**
Let's flip to licensing, because Vertex has been busy there too and it's a completely different posture.

April 7th this year. Vertex signs a global exclusive collaboration and license with Halozyme for their Hypercon technology. Up to three drug targets. Fifteen million dollars upfront, plus milestones and royalties on net sales.

Hypercon is a microparticle thing — it lets you hyperconcentrate a biologic so you can deliver the same dose in a much smaller injection. Which is what makes at-home dosing possible instead of dragging patients into an infusion center.

**ISSA**
Fifteen million.

**CHINMAY**
Fifteen million.

**ISSA**
Put that next to the last segment for a second. That's about a sixth of one percent of what they just paid for Crinetics.

And here's what I love about it. Fifteen million is *exactly* the median upfront for a discovery-stage deal in our data. Dead on the median. Vertex paid the going rate for one preclinical program, and got a platform across three targets for it.

**CHINMAY**
So is it a rounding error or is it strategic?

**ISSA**
Both, and that's the point of a deal like this.

What you're buying isn't the technology. You're buying the *right* to use the technology, at a price low enough that you don't have to be correct. All the real money is pushed out into milestones and royalties, which means Halozyme only gets paid if Vertex actually succeeds with it. Risk sits with the party who controls the outcome. That's how these should be built and a lot of them aren't.

And I'd contrast it directly with Crinetics, where Vertex took every ounce of risk onto their own balance sheet on day one. Same company, same twelve months, completely opposite posture. Both right, because the two assets are at opposite ends of how much anybody actually knows.

**CHINMAY**
Are more deals getting structured this way, or is this a Vertex thing?

**ISSA**
More deals, definitely. Option structures were about eight percent of the transactions in our set back in 2022. They're around eighteen percent now. More than doubled in four years. Everybody's figured out that you can rent conviction instead of buying it.

**CHINMAY**
What does it tell you about where Vertex is going?

**ISSA**
This is the part I like. Vertex is a small-molecule house. Historically. Cystic fibrosis, the pain program, it's chemistry, it's pills.

You don't go license high-concentration *biologics* delivery for a portfolio you don't have.

**CHINMAY**
Povetacicept's a biologic.

**ISSA**
Povetacicept's a fusion protein, right. So read it forward. They're building the plumbing for a future portfolio that's injectable, chronic, and self-administered at home. And they bought that option for fifteen million dollars, years before they need it.

Halozyme's pretty explicit that Hypercon sits alongside ENHANZE rather than replacing it, so this isn't Vertex swapping platforms. It's them putting a marker down.

Cheapest strategic option anybody bought this year, I'd say.

## ONO — Japan and Korea

**CHINMAY**
Back up to 2025 for the last two, because they're the same molecule.

June 23rd last year, Vertex and Ono Pharmaceutical sign an exclusive license for povetacicept in Japan and South Korea. IgA nephropathy plus other B-cell diseases. Ono helps run the clinical work, owns the marketing authorizations, and commercializes in both markets once it's approved.

And the money flows toward Vertex — upfront, regulatory and commercial milestones, tiered royalties. Terms weren't disclosed.

**ISSA**
And this is where it gets fun, because you have to remember where povetacicept came from.

Vertex paid four point nine billion for Alpine Immune Sciences in 2024. Povetacicept *was* the reason for that deal. It's the whole asset.

**CHINMAY**
So they buy it for almost five billion and then hand pieces of it away.

**ISSA**
They don't hand it away, they rent out the rooms they weren't going to live in. Vertex was never going to build a nephrology field force in Japan. Never. So the choice is: leave the territory sitting there doing nothing, or turn it into cash coming in plus a royalty stream, and keep the US and Europe, which is where almost all the value is anyway.

That's just good portfolio management. It's not a retreat and I'd hate for anyone to read it that way.

**CHINMAY**
Why Japan specifically though? Feels like a small market for a kidney drug.

**ISSA**
It's the opposite, actually, and this is a nice bit of trivia.

Japan does mandatory urinalysis screening through the school system. Has for decades. And the literature says something like seventy to eighty percent of detected mesangial proliferative glomerulonephritis gets picked up through that program.

**CHINMAY**
So they find it earlier than anybody.

**ISSA**
They find it earlier and more systematically than basically anywhere on earth. The patients are already identified. The nephrologists are concentrated. Ono can actually reach them. That's not a random territory, that's arguably the cleanest single market in the world for this drug.

**CHINMAY**
Anything to read into the terms being undisclosed?

**ISSA**
A little. When a regional license doesn't disclose, it's almost always a modest upfront with the weight sitting in milestones. And that's the shape across the board now — in our benchmarks milestones run sixty to seventy-five percent of total potential deal value, and the ratio of total value to upfront has stretched from about eight-to-ten times back in 2022 to ten-to-fourteen times today.

**CHINMAY**
So can you actually put a number on what Ono paid?

**ISSA**
Not honestly, no, and I want to be straight about that. Our Phase 3 licensing benchmark is a hundred and fifty million median on the upfront, but that's for *global* rights. Ono got two countries. Regional deals price way underneath the global comps and anybody quoting you the global median for a Japan-and-Korea license is selling you something.

What the benchmark does tell you is the *shape*. Small upfront, long milestone tail. Which is right, honestly — your partner is absorbing development and regulatory risk in their own market, so you let them pay you as they de-risk it. Ono's buying a shot on goal. Vertex is selling square footage it wasn't using.

## ZAI LAB — Greater China

**CHINMAY**
And then the other one, which actually came first. January 10th, 2025. Same molecule, same structure, different geography — Zai Lab gets mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan and Singapore. Upfront to Vertex, regulatory milestones, tiered royalties.

**ISSA**
Notice which direction this deal points.

**CHINMAY**
Meaning?

**ISSA**
For three years now the entire story in this industry has been Western pharma *in*-licensing Chinese assets. Cheap molecules, fast enrollment, NewCo structures, everybody's doing it, it's all anyone talks about at JPM.

This is the opposite. This is a Western company out-licensing into China. And that's rarer than people think.

**CHINMAY**
Is China a big enough market to matter here?

**ISSA**
For IgA nephropathy it's the biggest one there is. Largest absolute patient population in the world, and it's the most common biopsy-proven glomerulonephritis there by a wide margin. This is not a throwaway.

And Zai Lab has become sort of the default counterparty for this trade, because they've built regulatory and commercial infrastructure that a US biotech genuinely cannot replicate on any timeline that matters.

**CHINMAY**
So put Ono and Zai Lab together for me.

**ISSA**
Yeah, don't read them as two deals. Read them as one move.

In about five months Vertex regionalized an entire acquired asset — Greater China, Singapore, Japan, Korea — and kept the US and Europe outright.

So the model, and I really want founders to hear this one: buy the global asset, keep the two markets that carry the economics, and monetize everything else for cash and speed and local execution. Every one of those regional deals quietly chips away at what Alpine actually cost them, and they didn't give up a dollar of the core to do it.

When a company does that twice in a year with the same molecule, that's not opportunism. There's a memo behind that.

## CLOSE

**CHINMAY**
Alright, tie it together. Four deals. What's the thread?

**ISSA**
The thread is that Vertex is really disciplined about where they'll put principal at risk.

They wrote one enormous check. All cash, no contingency, huge premium, no competing bidder — for the one thing they had total internal conviction about. And everywhere the conviction was lower, they refused to risk capital. Fifteen million for the Halozyme option. And on the two Asia deals they weren't paying at all, they were collecting.

**CHINMAY**
Cash where they're sure, structure where they're not.

**ISSA**
That's it. And in a year with fifty-plus deals, that's rarer than it should be. A lot of buyers do it backwards — they hedge the thing they're certain about and go all-in on the thing they're guessing at.

**CHINMAY**
What are you watching from here?

**ISSA**
Three things, quickly.

The first post-close quarter, mostly to see how they book it, because that one accounting decision determines whether Vertex's GAAP numbers are comparable to anything for the next two years.

The term loan. Watch when they draw it and watch how fast they kill that 364-day tranche. That tells you how confident they really are about cash generation.

And whether endocrinology turns into a program or stays a one-off. One deal's a pillar. Two deals is a strategy.

**CHINMAY**
And if you're a founder listening to this trying to figure out where you sit?

**ISSA**
Then the answer's in the comps, and I mean that literally.

Which buyers actually paid cash. Where CVRs showed up and what triggered them. What the premium looks like when there's a real auction versus when there's one bidder in the room. What upfront-to-total runs at your stage, in your indication, for your modality.

That's the whole reason we built Solidus. Every number I've quoted today came out of it, and every one of them traces back to a filing.

**CHINMAY**
Where do people find it?

**ISSA**
solidus dot ambrosiaventures dot co. It's free to start.

And look, the reason it matters — upfronts are up twenty to thirty percent across every stage since 2022. If you're negotiating off a comp you remember from a few years ago, you are negotiating against yourself. There's a real difference between walking into a BD meeting with a number, and walking in with a number you can defend for forty-five minutes.

**CHINMAY**
Good place to end it. That's Vertex. Thanks Issa.

**ISSA**
Anytime.

**CHINMAY**
Catch us on the next segment of Hot Off The Press.
